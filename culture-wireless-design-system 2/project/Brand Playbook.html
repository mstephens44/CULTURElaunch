01Introduction
A letter from the team

Born from a collective vision.

Culture Wireless™ was created in direct response to the systemic inequities in broadband access faced by underserved communities. Launched by veteran entrepreneurs, community organizers, and technologists, we exist to close the digital divide for good — starting with those most often left behind.

This playbook is the operating manual for how we show up — in language, in design, in the field, and at the register. It is the source of truth for every team member, partner, vendor, and ambassador acting on behalf of the brand. When in doubt, return here.

Treat the work seriously. Treat the communities we serve more seriously still.

Who we serve

Public housing residents, rural communities, military families, and low-income neighborhoods — the unserved, the underserved, and the unconnected.

The promise

Fast, affordable internet delivered with the dignity our communities deserve — never as charity, always as infrastructure.

03Audience
Two minds, one mission

Connect to the consumer.

Every campaign we run is read by two distinct minds. We never speak to a generic "user." We speak to one of these two — and we know the difference.

Persona 01 Cause-conscious
Missionaries

Believers in dignity-as-infrastructure. They sign up because the mission resonates — they want their dollar to vote for the kind of company that serves the block, not the boardroom. They forgive a bumpy onboarding if the story is true.

Reach them through neighborhood organizations, faith leaders, civic events, and earned media. Show real people, real homes, real consequences.

Persona 02 Price-conscious
Mercenaries

Pragmatists. They've been burned by hidden fees and 24-month traps. They want a number they can trust and a speed that runs the household. The mission is a bonus — the price has to win first.

Reach them through direct comparison: speed tiers, flat price, no contract. Show the bill. Show the router. Show the door-to-door installer who actually knows their name.

05SWOT
An honest map

SWOT analysis.

S

Strengths

  • Deep community trust — built by founders who live in the markets we serve.
  • Mission-aligned investor base and civic partnerships unlock buildings competitors can't.
  • Lean ops & modern fixed-wireless stack — faster deployment, lower cost per home.
  • Brand resonance with public housing, military, and rural audiences underserved by incumbents.
W

Weaknesses

  • Smaller field footprint than national carriers — coverage gaps outside priority zones.
  • Brand awareness still low outside launch markets.
  • Limited self-service tooling; support volume scales with subscribers.
  • Capex-heavy market entry slows speed-to-launch in new geographies.
O

Opportunities

  • Federal & state digital-equity funding aligns directly with our charter.
  • Multi-family operators want a partner who can wire a building and earn the tenants' trust.
  • Small-business backhaul gap in our markets is wide open.
  • Influencer & faith-leader networks ready to co-brand at low cost.
T

Threats

  • Incumbents with deeper pockets running predatory promo pricing.
  • Spectrum policy & subsidy programs subject to political reversal.
  • Supply-chain delays on CPE and antennas.
  • "Charity-brand" perception risk if positioning is not handled with care.

Influencer & partnerships.

Our most credible spokespeople already live on the blocks we serve. Faith leaders, barbershop owners, neighborhood organizers, school principals, local creators — they earn trust we have to borrow. Treat that borrowed trust like the asset it is.

Tiers of partnership

Civic anchorFaith centers, housing authorities, libraries — joint programs, no fee-based promotion.
Community voiceLocal creators & organizers — paid posts, scripted by them, approved by us.
Co-brandMulti-family operators, small businesses — joint plans, in-building signage.
AffiliateDoor-to-door referrers — Culture Connectors program, paid per activated home.

The five principles

  • Mutually beneficialBoth sides walk away with a clear win — not just our logo on their flyer.
  • Shared objectivesAgreed before the first asset ships. KPI lives in the deal memo.
  • Joint creativeWe never hand over a script. We co-write, then approve.
  • Co-branded standardsLogo lockup, voice, and color treatment locked in writing.
  • Affiliate transparencyIf a Connector earns a fee, it is disclosed in the post. No exceptions.

Approval window

All partner-facing creative routes through Brand within 72 hours. Past 72: assume yes, but log it.

10Agreement
Get it on paper

Partnership agreement.

Every partnership — civic, paid, or co-branded — starts with a one-page deal memo. Verbal agreements don't count. The memo lives in our shared drive before the first asset is produced.

What the memo must cover

  • Parties & primary contactsLegal name, point of contact, escalation contact.
  • Shared objectiveOne sentence. The thing we're both trying to make true.
  • Scope & deliverablesQuantity, channels, dates, ownership of final assets.
  • CompensationFlat fee, performance, in-kind, equity, or none. Spelled out.
  • Brand & usage rightsWhere each party's marks may appear, and for how long.
  • Exit & review30-day check-in, sunset clause, who keeps what.

Co-branded creative

Always show both wordmarks at equal weight. The Culture Wireless wordmark appears in the signature gradient — never in partner colors. Partner logo appears in its approved color. Minimum 32px clear-space around our lockup.

Joint marketing

Co-funded campaigns track to one shared KPI agreed in the memo. Each partner reports back at day 30 and day 90. No surprises.

Affiliate program

Culture Connectors earn per activated home: $30 residential, $100 small business. Paid monthly. Disclosed on every post per FTC guidance.

12Field Kit
Show up ready

Field kit & activation playbook.

The crate

Every regional crew keeps a sealed event crate ready to roll. Inventoried after each event, restocked within 48 hours. Don't break the seal until load-in.

Structure

  • 10×10 pop-up tent, navy
  • 8 ft folding table + skirt
  • 2 retractable banners (gradient, navy)
  • 1 step-and-repeat 8×8 ft
  • 2 director's chairs (navy)
  • Floor decals (3 ×)

Tech

  • 2 iPads w/ Culture POS
  • 1 BR-1 Mini 5G (backhaul)
  • 1 demo router + customer display
  • 2 portable battery packs
  • Card reader + chargers
  • Backup hotspot

Collateral

  • Plan one-sheeters (×100 ea)
  • ACP enrollment guide (×50)
  • Connectors referral cards (×100)
  • Business cards · regional
  • Spanish & English variants

Hospitality

  • Water cooler + cups
  • Branded stickers (kids)
  • USB-C cables (give-away)
  • First-aid kit, sunscreen
  • Cash float — $200 in 5s & 10s
Rule

Anything we give away has our wordmark on it and is genuinely useful. No throwaway swag. Stress-test it on a teenager — if it's in the trash by sundown, kill the SKU.

The 14-day activation plan

Every event runs to this clock. Skip a step, miss a connection.

  • T−14 · Site visit & permitWalk the venue. Confirm power, footprint, and load-in window. Lock permit.
  • T−10 · Crew & partner briefOne-page brief: offer, KPI, key partner contacts, escalation chain.
  • T−7 · Local promotionGeo-targeted social, partner newsletters, neighborhood flyers — never sooner.
  • T−2 · Kit pull & QAOpen the crate. Charge every device. Run the POS in airplane-mode rehearsal.
  • T−0 · Load-in (90 min)Tent up, table dressed, signage hung, demo running — well before doors open.
  • Event day · The pulseHourly check-ins. Track sign-ups, ACP qualifiers, leads. Restock collateral.
  • T+1 · Lead follow-upEvery lead contacted within 24 hours by a human. Voice-first, text-second.
  • T+5 · Debrief & restock15-minute crew debrief. What worked, what didn't, what to fix. Crate resealed.
